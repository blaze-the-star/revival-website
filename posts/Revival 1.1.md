Everything felt *off*.  
He knew that before anything else.  
Though he wasn't even conscious yet—he just…  

*Knew.*  

Sound crept in, surfacing from the darkness.  
Faint impressions of swaying, rustling, a world breathing.  
Around him were voices, echoing along the distant edge of perception.  

Thought came next—unfocused—reaching.

> _This isn't my home._  
> _Where am I?_  

The outside voices rattled on, falling flat against the surface of his mind.  
He tightened his concentration, focusing in on the words.  

There was a young voice, hushed and urgent.  

> “You think he's civil?”  

A steadier one—feminine.  

> “He's wild.”  

Another—low and rough.  

> “And what do you plan on doing with him?”  
> “If he dies, he dies. Nothing we can do about it.”  

Concern ran through their voices, but thin—like tending a sick dog.  

He shifted on what he expected to be a firm spring mattress under soft covers.  
Instead, the surface beneath him was coarse and gritty, and his cover was rough and weathered.  

> _This isn't my cover or my bed._  
> _What's going on?_  
> _Am I dreaming?_

He inhaled—
And the smells hit all at once.
Damp earth, rotting leaves, and something warm—close, living.
They were wild, unfamiliar, and all of them too strong.

His breath caught as his mind tried—and failed—to make sense of it.

> “He's stirring,” said the rough voice.  

Then the steady one:  

> “Chatter, come'ere.”  
> “If he bolts, let him.”  

He raised a hand to rub his eyes, but when it got close—  

*Bonk.*  

> “Nyeh,” he moaned.  

His hand hit his face too soon.  
He moved his hand again with more care and he felt it—something long and thin.  
It was his face, but it was shaped wrong.  

Then he opened his eyes to find his hand… wasn't a hand.  
He held it out in front of him.  

Claws.  
Beans.  
Fur.  

_A paw._  

He stared at it, waiting for the image to correct itself.  

It didn't  

The young one swallowed audibly.  

> “…Snout.”  

The rough voice answered next, low and puzzled, as if trying to make sense of the strange behavior.  

> “He don’t recognize himself.”  

He tore his gaze from his would-be hand and looked at the ones talking.  

Old-style clothes.  
Simple.  
Browns, tans, and greys.  

Then he realized what they were.  

A coyote crouched low in concern.  
An otter leaned in with eyes like golf-balls.  
A bobcat stood upright behind them, arms crossed—analyzing.  

He couldn't help but feel how small he was surrounded by them.  

The coyote extended a paw toward him, a gentle signal to stay still.  

> “Easy now.”  
> “Can ya' understand me?”  

He blinked and tilted his head.  

> “Uh… Yeah?…” he mumbled.  

The coyote lowered her paw, nodding.  

> “I'm Snout.”  
> “And you?”  

> “I- I'm Joseph.”  
> “What's going on?”  
> “Where am I?”  

The otter's whiskers twitched.  

> “That ain't a local name.”  

> “No, it ain't,” replied the bobcat.  

> “You're in the Spine Weaver forest.”  
> “A day's walk to Flint Water,” Snout finished.  

Joseph stared with blinking wide eyes—mouth slightly agape.  

> “…Huh?”  

They all went quiet.  

The otter stepped forward.  

> “You don't know Flint Water?”  

> “Every _fox_ within three rivers knows Flint,” said the bobcat.  

> _Fox?_  

The word snagged.  

> _Is that what I am?_  

Snout chimed in, voice low.  

> “What do you remember?”
> “Anything before this?”

> “Like, where's your clothes?” the otter chimed in.  

Joseph blinked, then examined himself.  
The cover had slid below his shoulders, and he was wearing nothing but fur.  

> “I-I-”  

He stammered, pulling the cover to his neck—discretely.  

> “I don't know.”  
> “The last thing I remember, I was home in bed.”  

Joseph swallowed.  

> “I don't remember *anything* strange happening before waking up here.”  

Everyone went silent again.  
The coyote and bobcat exchanged glances, and the otter looked between them.  

> “Could be lyin',” said the bobcat.  

Snout’s gaze sharpened immediately.  

The bobcat huffed and raised his paws.  

> “Whatever, I don't know how to handle this.”  
> “I'm getting Eli.”  

He walked into the brush—his absence leaving the lingering sound of whispering trees.  
A pressing silence.  

Snout exhaled slowly through her nose, her posture easing just a fraction.  
Her gaze shifted, flicking briefly to the otter.  

Chatter leaned in closer to her, lowering his voice without meaning to.  

> “I mean—if he’s not lyin’, then what does that even—”  

> “We'll figure that out,” Snout said, steady.  

Not looking at Joseph now—thinking.

Joseph was becoming increasingly aware of himself.  

Low to the ground, folded in on himself, surrounded by bodies that stood upright where he _should_ have been standing.  

 But he wasn’t.  

He felt small—
Vulnerable.

Something in his chest tightened.  

He pulled the rough cover tighter around himself, clutching it with both paws.  
The motion felt clumsy, imprecise—like trying to tie knots with numb fingers.  

He leaned forward, propped himself against his arms, and pushed up.  

His limbs didn’t line up right.  
His weight tipped too far forward, then too far back.  
His shoulders hunched instinctively, spine curling where it shouldn’t.  

Still—he pushed.  
His legs straightened.  
For a moment—just a moment—he rose.  
Too tall, unsteady, wrong.  

He looked ahead where the otter’s voice carried on, half-whispered:  

> “I mean, you think he’s telling the truth, or—”  

He tilted.  

_Thud._

Joseph hit the ground—breath knocked out of him.  

The cover wrapped his limbs.  
One of his legs kicked uselessly against it, not bending the way he expected—joint catching at an angle that made no sense in his mind.  

The otter and coyote turned their heads.  

> “…what was that?” said the otter.  

Joseph sucked in air, sharp and uneven. 

_No. Get up. Come on—get up._  

He shifted till the cover no longer restricted him and rose.  
This time faster, more desperate.  
He pushed against his arms again, forcing his body upward—but everything resisted.  
His balance pitched forward immediately, his weight sitting in the wrong places, his center pulled somewhere beneath his chest instead of above his hips.  

His back twisted, and his legs locked.  
For a fraction of a second, he made it higher than before—  

Then everything slipped.  

He collapsed again—harder this time, shoulder striking first, then the side of his face against the grit.  

The world jolted.  

Joseph lay there, stunned—air dragging back into his lungs in shallow, uneven pulls.  

Then he planted his paws again.  
Tried to push himself up—  

> “Stop.”  

Snout’s voice cut through—firm—right beside him now.  

> “You’re going to hurt yourself.”  

Joseph froze, halfway lifted.  
His body wavered—uncertain whether to obey her or the instinct clawing at him to _fix this_.  

> “What’s wrong?” she asked, steady—non-accusing.  
> “What’s not working?”

Joseph swallowed, breath hitching.  

> “I—”  

The words didn’t come.  
He glanced down at himself—at the limbs that bent wrong, the weight that sat wrong, the balance that refused to exist.  

How do you explain inheriting the shape of a different body?  

> “I can’t—”  

He stopped.  
Shook his head.  
It wasn’t that he couldn’t stand, not exactly.  
It was—  

> _This is wrong._  

> “I don’t know,” he said instead, voice thin.  

Chatter shifted closer, eyes darting over him.  

> “You mean you’re hurt, or—like—you can’t feel your legs, or—”  

> “I can feel them,” Joseph said, quick.  
> “That’s not—” he cut himself off, shaking his head.  

He tried again to adjust his footing—just to sit higher—but his arms bent weird and he slipped, forcing him back down.  
Frustration flared sharp in his chest.  

> _Why can’t I—_  

The brush rustled.  
All three of them turned.  

The bobcat pushed through first—but Joseph barely registered him.  
The one behind him drew his attention just by his presence.  

A red wolf stepped into the clearing.  
Muted reds along his coat, darkened where the light didn’t catch.  
Pale scars crossed his face—old, clean lines that didn’t fade into the fur.  
He carried himself differently from the others—no hesitation, no adjustment to the space.  

Joseph felt the difference immediately.  
Not just taller, but solid in a way the others weren’t.  

Joseph’s chest tightened.  
He felt small before, but now?...  

The wolf’s gaze passed over the group once—quick, efficient—then settled on Joseph.  

> “That him?” he asked.  

> “That’s him,” the bobcat—said.  
> “Doesn’t know where he is. Says he just woke.”  

The wolf stepped closer.  
Measured, unhurried.  

> “You remember anything?” he asked.  

Joseph hesitated.  

> “I- No- I don't.”  

> “Scruff said you were convulsing when they found you,” the wolf said.  
> “Crying out.”  

Joseph blinked.  

> “…I— I don’t remember that.”  

> “Mm.”  

The wolf lowered—not enough to match Joseph, not even close, but just enough to close the distance.  

> “No clothes. No memory. You don’t know where you are.”  
> “That about right?”  

Joseph nodded faintly.  

The wolf studied him a moment longer.  

> “_Something’s_ wrong,” he stated.  

Joseph stiffened.  

> “I’m fine,” he said, too quickly.  

Snout’s gaze snapped back to him.  

> “No, you’re not.”  

The bobcat huffed.  

> “His head ain't right.”  

Snout’s ears flicked back.  

> “Don’t start, Scruff.”  

> “I’m sayin’ what it is.”  

> “You don’t know that.”  

> “Look at him.”  

> “I see him.”  

Their voices didn’t rise—but the edge was there.  

Joseph felt it building around him.  

> “Fox was shaking in the dirt,” Scruff went on.  
> “Now he says he’s fine?”  

> “He’s disoriented,” Snout said.  
> “That’s not the same thing.”  

> “Or he’s lost it.”  

That landed.  
Something in Joseph twisted.  

> “That’s enough,” Eli snapped at the two.  

Then, to Joseph:

> “You don’t want to say it.”  

Joseph’s ears flicked.  

> “…You wouldn’t get it,” he muttered.  

The words hung there alone for a moment, then Eli spoke again:  

> “Try me.”  

Joseph shook his head.  
Harder this time.  

> “I- I just—”  

He looked down again.  
At his paws.  
At the ground.  
At anything but them.  

> “It’s wrong,” he said.  

His voice thin, barely there.  
The silence stretched—tightened.  

Then—  

> “It’s not right,” he forced out.
> “_Everything’s_ wrong—this—”  

He motioned at himself, sudden, frantic. 

> “I’m not—this—”  

He shook his head sharply.  

> “I’m not a fox!”  

He dragged in another breath.  

> “I’m— I’m human.”  

Silence.  

Chatter blinked.  

> “…A what?”  

Snout frowned, trying to follow.  

> “You’re… saying you’re not a fox…”  

Careful, grounding.  

Scruff didn’t hesitate.  

> “His head _really_ ain't right.”  

> “Stop,” Snout snapped, sharper now.  

> “What, you believe that?”  

> “I didn’t say that.”  

> “Then what are you sayin’?”  

> “Don’t push him right now.”  

Joseph’s chest tightened.  

Their voices blurred.  
They remained tempered, but pressed in regardless.  
On him—around him—through him.  

He didn’t have anything left to give them.  
The words—the explanations—none of it mattered.  

He dropped back down, folding in on himself—paws pressing hard against his eyes.  
Just… done.  

The wolf straightened and silence settled again, different now.  

> “Snout, Scruff, Chatter.”  

They looked up immediately.  

> “Find him something to wear.”  

Snout held his gaze for a moment, then nodded once.  

> “…Alright.”  

Chatter glanced once more at Joseph—hesitating—but followed.  

Scruff lingered half a second longer.  
Then turned without a word.  

The three of them moved off through the brush—quiet.  
They understood.  

The clearing stilled.  

Joseph didn’t move.  

The wolf stepped closer, and paused.  
Then extended a hand—a paw.  

> “Hey.”  

No response.  

> “You’re not staying down there.”  
> “Do you want help, or not?”  

Slowly, he lowered his paws from his eyes.  
He looked at the paw, then up at him.  
That same steady, unreadable expression.  

Waiting.  

Joseph swallowed.  

> “…Yeah.”  

Quiet, reluctant.  

He shifted, clutching the rough cover tight around himself with one paw as he reached out with the other.  
Their hands met in a firm grasp.  

Joseph pulled.  
His legs wobbled immediately, balance pitching—but the wolf adjusted, guiding him.  

Joseph held the cover tighter around himself as he rose to his feet.  
His legs quivered, but he stood—he was upright.  
He froze there, breath tight.  
Waiting for the fall—  

But Eli's hand held him in place.  

> “Easy,” the wolf said.  

After a moment, the grip eased—just slightly.  

Joseph swayed, focused—finding where his weight actually was.  

The others returned, brush shifting as they stepped back into the clearing—bundles of cloth in their arms.  
They slowed when they saw him standing.  

Snout’s eyes flicked from Joseph to the wolf—taking it in.  

> “…He’s up.”  

The wolf didn’t look away from Joseph and simply nodded.  

> “He’s staying with us.”  
> “We'll camp here till he's ready to move.”  

Scruff’s ears snapped forward.  

> “You’re serious?”  

> “He’s not walking out of here like that,” the wolf said.  

Scruff frowned.  

> “We don’t know what he is.”  

> “We know enough,” the wolf replied.  
> “He was alone. No supplies. No memory.”  
> “You want to leave him out here like this?”  

Scruff’s jaw shifted.  
No answer.  

Snout stepped in before it could turn.  

> “We’ll make it work,” she said.  

Chatter nodded quickly.  

> “Yeah—yeah, we can—we can do it—”  

Joseph didn’t hear most of it.  
His focus had narrowed again—down to the ground beneath him.  
To the way his weight shifted.  
To how wrong it still felt.  

But—  

Carefully, he shifted one paw forward.  
It landed uneven.  
His weight followed late and his body lurched—  
He caught himself.  
Adjusted.  

Another step.  
Clumsy—unsteady.  

He was walking—  
that was real—  
even if nothing else made sense.
