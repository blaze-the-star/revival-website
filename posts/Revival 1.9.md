(Revision: Pearl is now called Soot.)

Flint Water was quieter in the evening.  
  
A town settling—just settling.  
Stalls were closing, voices lowering.  
The last drops of light thinned, and the river turned to a dull, reflective sheen that made distance hard to judge.  
  
Joseph walked ahead of the others, claws scratching lightly against the packed earth, ears half-cocked for footsteps that weren’t there.  
  
He glanced back.  
  
Snout was a few paces behind him, eyes forward, unhurried.  
Chatter ambled alongside her, tail swaying, clearly content to let someone else lead for once.  
  
> “Why am I in front?” Joseph asked as if it needed justification.  
  
Snout didn’t answer right away.  
  
> “Because,” she said at last,  
> “I don’t want to lose you again.”  
  
He frowned, but did as he was told.  
  
> “I don’t even know the way.”  
  
Snout’s voice came easily from behind him.  
  
> “You don’t need to.”  
  
> “That’s not reassuring.”  
  
She made a soft, amused sound.  
  
> “Left here.”  
  
Joseph turned, then sighed.  
  
> “Where are we going, anyway?”  
  
> “The Gathered Bowl.”  
  
He hesitated mid-step.  
  
> “The- huh?”  
  
> “A place we can eat,” Chatter said helpfully.  
> “Food you don’t have to cook yourself.”  
  
Snout nodded.  
  
> “Thought you could use a break from camp,” she added.  
> “Somewhere with heat. And people.”  
  
Joseph made a face.  
  
> “I’ve been around people.”  
  
> “You’ve been around us,” Snout said.  
> “That doesn’t count.”  
  
He grumbled, but left it at that.  
  
They walked on in quiet companionship.  
Snout called out directions as needed, until the street began to slope gently downward toward the river.  
The smell reached him before the building did—stew, grain, and smoke braided together in a way that made his stomach tighten despite himself.  
  
Tucked between a cooper’s shop and a chandler’s stall sat **The Gathered Bowl**.  
Smoke curled from its chimney, gently fading into the sky.  
  
Snout *finally* walked ahead, entering the establishment.  
Joseph fell back a step without thinking.  
  
Inside, long wooden tables ran the length of the room, crowded with animals of all kinds.  
There were no private booths—just shared space and the low, warm hum of community.  
The evening sun had little effect through the windows.
The brightest light came from lanterns mounted on the walls, but they were too sparse to meaningfully light up the room.  
Instead, candles on the tables were what gave flickers of sight to those around the them.  
  
Joseph paused in the doorway, his ears tilting.  
  
> “We’re… sitting with strangers?”  
  
> “Of course,” Snout said. “Why not?”  
  
> “Where I’m from, you usually sit alone. With your own group.”  
  
Chatter blinked up at him.  
  
> “Really? That sounds lonely.”  
  
Snout shrugged.  
  
> “Different ways. Here, you share space unless there’s a fight brewing.”  
  
She chose a spot near the middle where a pair of older badgers and a young raccoon were already eating.  

Snout settled onto a floor cushion and Chatter pulled a stool from under the table, hopping onto it with otter-ish grace.  
  
Joseph pulled himself a stool across from them and eyed the thing.  
It looked cobbled together, and it balanced unevenly.  
With careful paws, he climbed onto it, tail coiled for balance.  
Even seated, he was shorter than Snout.  
He felt perched like a child in a high-chair.  
  
Chatter was already settled in.  
He leaned toward the nearest badger.  
  
> “What’s in the stew today?”  
  
> “Roots, river fish, thyme,” the badger grunted.  
> “Tastes better than it sounds.”  
  
Joseph kept his gaze down, adjusting his coat sleeves. That’s when a scent caught him—warm earth, frost, that faint fruity sweetness.  
  
His head turned before he could stop it.  
  
There, one table over, stood Crystal.  
The red fox was watching him.  
When their eyes met, she didn’t look away—just tilted her head slightly.  
  
Joseph twitched, and the stool shifted under him.  
One leg lifted off the floor, and he jerked forward, clutching the table edge.  

He didn’t look back again.  
  
> “You alright?” Snout asked, her voice low.  
  
> “Fine,” Joseph muttered. “Stool’s uneven.”  
  
> “They all are,” said one of the badgers without looking up.  
> “Builds character.”  
  
A moment later, a shadow fell across their corner of the table.  
Crystal pulled over a stool from another spot and set it down beside Joseph—close enough that her sleeve brushed his.  
  
She sat as though she’d been invited.  
  
Joseph stared rigidly at the empty space in front of him.  
Chatter’s eyes went wide.  
  
> “Joseph—isn't that the fox from the alley!”  
  
Joseph’s ears flattened as he shot Chatter a glare that could've cut ice.  
  
Snout’s gaze sharpened.  
  
> “The alley?”  
  
> “It was nothing,” Joseph said too quickly.  
> “I dropped my blanket. *She gave it back.*”  
  
Crystal glanced at him, caught off guard—then smoothed her expression and studied the wall as if it were fascinating.  
  
Snout looked between them, her coyote-smile slow and knowing.  
  
> “Ohhh.”  
  
Joseph blinked.  
  
> “Why’d you say it like that?” He hissed.  
  
> “Just noting,” Snout replied mildly.  
> “Most folks wouldn’t have done that.”  
  
Joseph blinked, then darted his eyes around the table.  
The badgers were pretending not to listen.  
The raccoon had paused, spoon halfway to his mouth.  
  
> “Why do I feel like there’s something you’re not telling me?”  
  
Crystal covered her mouth with one paw, a quiet laugh escaping.  
  
A server stopped at their table, a young fox.  
He set bowls of red stew before them along with loafs of bread.  
With everyone and their meal before them, Crystal turned her attention away from Joseph and toward Snout instead.  
  
> “So,” she said, conversational,  
> “You're one of Eli's guards?”  
  
Snout perked a little at that, glad for the opening.  
  
> “Why, yes. I am. For almost four years, now.”  
> “Longer, if you count before it was official.”  
  
> “Yeah,” Chatter added.  
> “Back when I was still more trouble than help.”  
  
Snout snorted.  
  
> “Still are.”  
  
Joseph focused on his food.  
He dipped some bread in the bowl and chewed it.  
  
Crystal listened as Snout talked—about patrol routes, about camps, about places that were quieter than they used to be.  
She asked the sort of questions that kept the story moving, her attention steady and warm.  
Chatter chimed in where he could, animated, clearly enjoying himself.  
  
Joseph stayed still.  
  
Too still.  
  
Crystal glanced at him.  
  
> “You don’t don't say much, do you?” she asked.  
  
Not to Snout.  
To him.  
  
Snout’s voice trailed off mid-sentence.  
Chatter paused, spoon hovering, then very deliberately went back to his bowl.  
  
Joseph’s ears twitched as he barely lifted his head to speak.  
  
> “I don’t have anything to add,” he replied.  
  
Crystal hummed softly.  
  
>“That’s what you tell people.”  
  
He didn’t answer.  
Instead, he just dipped his head again.  
  
A moment passed.  
Then another.  
  
She turned back to Snout—but only briefly before she spoke again.  
  
> “You go very still when you don’t want to be seen.”  
  
Joseph frowned despite himself.  
  
> “I listen,” he said. “That’s it.”  
  
This time, she looked directly at him.  
  
> “You’ve been doing that since I sat down.”  
  
Something in Joseph’s chest tightened.  
  
> “That doesn’t mean I like you,” he cut in quick.  
  
The table stilled—enough that Joseph noticed how quiet his own breathing sounded.  
  
Crystal blinked once—then smiled, clearly amused despite his tone.  
  
> “I didn’t say you liked me.”  
  
Joseph felt heat creep into his ears.  
  
> “I—” He shook his head.  
> “And I didn’t say I did.”  
> “So—what’s your point?”  
  
Crystal’s smile softened, not retreating—just… settling.  
  
> “No point,” she said.  
> “I just like talking to you.”  
  
Joseph felt his fur stand on end.  
His heartbeat quickened.  
It felt like fear.  
But it wasn't.  
Not exactly.  
  
He shook his head again and took another bite of his bread.  
  
---  
  
Spoons scraped.  
Someone laughed farther down the table.  
The moment passed.  
  
Joseph ate.  
  
Carefully at first, then steadily, until the bowl was scraped clean and the bread gone with it.  
He set the spoon aside and leaned back slightly, tail curled around the leg of the stool.  
  
Conversation wandered.  
  
> “So,” Chatter said, mouth half-full,  
> “what d’you do when you’re not rescuin’ blankets from alleys?”  
  
Crystal glanced at him, amused.  
  
> “Archives,” she said.  
> “All day.”  
  
Snout hummed faintly.  
  
> “Records?”  
  
> “Transactions. Property logs. Old patrol notes.”  
> “Trade routes that don’t exist anymore.”  
> “Arguments that got settled three decades ago and nobody remembers why.”  
  
Joseph tilted his head slightly.  
  
> “Is there ever anything interesting in the archives?” he asked.  
  
Crystal answered without hesitation.  
  
> “Not usually,” she said.  
> “Just lists. Dates. Names that don’t matter anymore.”  
  
A pause.  
Her gaze lingered on him a fraction longer than it had before—curious.  
  
She shrugged, rolling one shoulder.  
  
> “History, mostly. The boring kind.”  
  
> “History’s important,” Chatter offered earnestly.  
  
> “It is,” Crystal said.  
> “Doesn’t make it interesting.”  
  
Snout nodded.  
  
> “Quiet work, at least,” she said.  
  
> “Usually,” Crystal replied.  
> “Though I’ve been hearing a lot of the same question lately.”  
  
> “How so?”  
  
> “Soot’s been asking around,” Crystal said easily, like it was nothing more than idle gossip.  
> She turned her head to Joseph.  
> “If anyone’s seen you. Where you went.”  
  
Joseph paused.  
  
> “Me?” He said confused.  
  
Crystal nodded.  
  
> “Mm. Just asking.”  
  
Snout’s voice stayed level.  
  
> “She ask you directly?”  
  
> “Not yet,” Crystal said.  
> “Just… listening.”  
  
Snout didn’t move.  
  
But Joseph saw it—the way her spine locked, the way one paw curled slightly against the table.  
  
Chatter cleared his throat.  
  
> “Soot’s always askin’ stuff,” he said.  
> “I think she just gets bored.”  
  
Crystal hummed in agreement.  
  
Snout didn’t.  
  
The rest of the meal passed quickly after that.  
  
> “I’ve got work early,” Crystal said.  
> “Archives don’t care if you’re tired.”  
  
She looked at Snout, nodded once.  
  
> “Good meeting you.”  
  
Then, softer—aimed somewhere between the table and Joseph—  
  
> “Stay warm.”  
  
Crystal handed marks to the waiter and she was gone, slipping back into the low-lit crowd.  
  
The group didn’t linger much longer, and they took their leave too.  
  
---  
  
The walk back through Flint Water was quieter than the walk there.  
The streets had thinned.  
Lanterns glowed dim against stone and wood.  
  
Chatter tried to fill the space anyway.  
  
> “Soot’s… uh—”  
> “She’s the herb fox, right?”  
> “The one with the fancy-looking sign?”  
  
Snout nodded.  
  
> “That’s her.”  
  
> “She always talks like she knows more than she does,” Chatter said.  
> “Drives Fight nuts.”  
  
They walked a bit farther.  
  
Joseph glanced at Snout.  
  
> “You went tense back there,” he said.  
> “When Crystal mentioned her.”  
  
Snout didn’t answer right away.  
  
The road crunched under their paws.  
Water murmured somewhere nearby.  
  
> “Soot doesn’t ask questions without reason,” she said.  
> “And I don’t know what reason she’d have for you.”  
  
Joseph frowned, uncertain.  
  
> “That doesn’t mean she wants to hurt anyone.”  
  
Snout stopped walking.  
  
Joseph and Chatter halted with her.  
  
> “I hope you’re right,” Snout said.  
> “But hope isn’t something I plan around.”  
  
She met Joseph’s eyes.  
  
> “She’s a thief. A troublemaker.”  
> “And she knows how to turn people into opportunities.” 
  
Joseph pressed.  
  
> “Are you sure you're right about her?”  
> “You said yourself there wasn't evidence to convict her.”  
  
Chatter shifted uncomfortably.  
  
> “She _is_ kind of a mess,” he offered.  
  
Snout nodded once.  
  
> “And messes get people hurt.”  
  
Joseph looked away, toward the dark line of trees beyond the road.  
  
He didn’t know what to think.  
There was no good reason to doubt Snout.  
Despite himself, something about it just felt... off.  
It didn't make any sense.  
  
_Am I just being overly optimistic?_  
  
Snout turned again toward camp.  
Chatter followed, then Joseph.  
  
Three shapes moving through the dark,  
with Flint Water settling quietly behind them,  
and questions following close at their heels.  
  
Behind them, the alley lay quiet.  
For a moment, a shape lingered at its edge—  
watching the road.  
Then it turned away, blending into the darkness.
