Joseph was sound asleep, snuggled comfortably beneath his cover, when voices stirred him awake.  
Muffled, unfamiliar. Urgent.

> “Found the lad lyin’ asleep in Chloros Forest, we did,” one said.

> “Chloros? Who in the deep-dark sleeps in a place like that, now?” another replied, disbelief thick in his voice.

Joseph’s mind crawled toward consciousness like molasses.  
The strange accents and speech patterns threw him for a loop.

_Am I having a stroke?_ he wondered groggily.

That was when he realized:  
He wasn’t in his bed.

The surface beneath him felt coarse… gritty… cold.  
He was lying outside.  
In the dirt.

_Where am I?_

His eyes cracked open.  
He tried looking around, but the sun stabbed straight into his face.  
He lifted an arm to shield his eyes, blinking hard.

> “Look there—he wakes,” said a feminine voice.

Shapes blurred into place—tents, tools, smoke, birdsong, the sharp smell of char and river air.  
Before he could process any of it, a wooden bowl was pushed toward him.

> “Here, we brought ya some water,” came a friendly young voice—boyish.

Joseph didn't think. He leaned toward the bowl—then froze.

The reflection in the water was not his own.  
Large pointed ears.  
A slim snout.  
Cheek fluff shifting with his breath.

_What is that…?_

He looked at it for a good few seconds, confused.  
Then it dawned on him.

The water rippled, revealing a slim snout, large pointed ears, and fluff pointing out from his cheeks.
Joseph’s breath hitched—not fear, exactly, more like his brain refusing to line up the pieces.
He stared a moment too long, waiting for the reflection to correct itself.

It didn’t.

> "...Hehe, oh no... I'm a furry." He said with a smile and flattened ears.

> “Huh?” said the young voice.

Joseph looked up at him. The first thing he saw were brown baggy pants then a simple but long olive-green shirt with buttons and long sleeves.

But instead of a human face…  
An otter blinked back at him, whiskers twitching in confusion.

> “He’s well outta sorts, ain’t he?” said some dry voice behind him.

Joseph looked back and cricked his head up to find a bobcat with crossed arms and a judging face next to a coyote with amber eyes narrowed and curious.

Joseph surly would've felt their heavy gazes beating down on him if he were aware enough to process it.

The otter piped up again, grabbing Joseph's attention.

> “Why wast thou out there, anyhow?”

Joseph blinked.

> “…Where?…” he murmured.

> “In the forest,” the boy prompted gently. “Where else?”

> “…What forest?…”

> “Chloros Forest,” he said again.  
> “Why’d thou wander in Chloros Forest?”

> “Uhhh…”

Joseph’s mind was a haze, hardly in any sort of helpful state.

The coyote and bobcat exchanged looks—the coyote’s concerned, the bobcat’s already done with this situation.

> “I ain’t dealin’ with this. I’m fetchin’ Eli,” the cat said before walking off.

Joseph yawned, head drifting down, eyes falling.

_This is a weird dream_, he thought.

The otter looked up at the coyote.

> “Reckon he were mugged?”

> “Nay sign of struggle,” she said gently. “Whoever took his things left him unharmed.”

The otter then found Joseph’s head on the ground, sleeping again.  
He nudged him insistently.

> “Hey! Hold thy horses—thou can’t sleep now! ’Tis the middle o’ the day!”

> “Ugh!…” Joseph protested.

He hoisted his swaying head off the ground when a shadow blanketed him, blotting out the sun.

### Interrogation

> “You there!”

Joseph snapped to full attention.  
Towering above him stood an **old red wolf**, grizzled and scarred, his presence radiating authority.  
The bobcat trailed just behind him.

> “Name,” the wolf demanded.

Joseph shrank back. His heart raced, words fumbling out in fragments.

> “It’s— I— Uh... Joseph.”

> “Joseph,” the elder repeated, softening his tone though still authoritative.

His gaze pierced down unrelenting.

> “’Twas told to me thou wert found senseless in Chloros Forest. What business had thee wanderin’ there alone?”

> “I... I don’t— I’m sorry, I don’t remember being in any forest,” Joseph stammered.  
> “I just remember going to sleep at home, then waking up here.”

As Joseph spoke he sat up, the cover sliding halfway down his chest.  

He froze — he wasn’t wearing anything beneath it.  
A jolt of panic shot through him.

_Why don’t I have clothes!?_

Discreetly as possible, he pulled the blanket back up to his neck, trying not to look suspicious.

The wolf didn't seem to notice.
That or he didn't care.  
He seemed preoccupied with finding answers.

He turned to the bobcat.

> “Fight. Fetch me a map.”

A map was spread out before Joseph—hand-drawn paths, landmarks, forests, towns.

> “Point to aught thou knowest,” the wolf instructed.

Joseph scanned the paper for anything he recognized, but really, he knew from the second he saw the map that he was not going to going to be able to identify anything.  
A puzzled look grew on his face with every second.

> “What, thou ne’er seen a map before?” the bobcat muttered.

> “Uhh… I- I don’t recognize any of this.”

The coyote leaned closer, eyes narrowing—trying to read Joseph’s expression.

The red wolf stared down at him in silence for a long moment.  
Not impatient this time—calculating.

The wolf’s brow furrowed slightly.

> “Joseph… what art thou?”

Joseph blinked, thrown by the question.  
He looked down at a paw—small, furred, fox-like—flexing it slightly as if they might reveal the answer.

Then he looked back up.

> “What kind of question is that?” he said, bewildered.

The wolf didn’t react, just continued watching him with quiet expectation.

Joseph hesitated, anxiety crawling up his spine.  
His mouth opened, then closed.  
He suddenly had no idea whether he was supposed to say _I’m a fox_ or… if he could even explain to them what he was.

He swallowed.

> “Can I, uh… ask a random question?” he managed.

The red wolf gave him a tight side-eye.  
Joseph could almost hear him say _It better not be stupid._

Joseph’s ears drooped.

> “Do you… Do you know what a human is?”

The world stopped.

The red wolf’s expression changed entirely—shock softening the hard lines in his face.

> “…You're human?”

> “…Well… Yes?” Joseph croaked.

Silence.

Then the old wolf exhaled slowly.

> “…Bless your soul.”

Fear prickled through Joseph’s fur.

> "W-what do you mean that? Why'd you say that!?"

The red wolf turned to the bobcat.

> “Ready an extra tent. Make certain we’ve rations fit for one more.”  
> “And find the lad some clothes.”

The bobcat flashed a too-large, too-forced grin.

> “Oh, joy…” he muttered.

Joseph sat there, stunned.  
His mind reeled so hard it could barely hold onto any single thought.

> “...Wait—what just happened?”

> “It’s thy lucky day!” The Tyrogue turned back with a strained grin.  
> “Ye get food an’ a place to sleep, free as rain!” He threw his arms wide with mock enthusiasm.  
> “’Cuz we’re just so darn generous, aye?”

He leaned in, dropping the sarcasm.

> “Also… what’s a human?”

### Reality

Surrounded by talking animals—fully clothed, fully aware, acting like people—Joseph stared at the dirt.

Everything felt unreal.

Barely above a whisper, he murmured:

> “…I’m not convinced any of this is real.”
