Everything felt *off*.
He knew that before anything else.
Though he wasn't even conscious yet—he just... 

*Knew.*

Sound crept in, surfacing from the darkness.
Faint impressions of swaying, rustling, a world breathing.
Accompanying it were voices—echoing in his mind without meaning.

Thought came next—sluggish, but trying to understand.

> _Am I dreaming?_
> _This is not my home._
> _Where am I?_

The outside voices rattled on, falling flat against the surface of his mind.
He tightened his concentration, focusing on the words.

A young voice, hushed and urgent.

> “You think he's civil?”  

A steadier one.

> “He's wild.”  

Another, low and rough.

> “And what do you plan on doing with him?”  
> “If he dies, he dies. Nothing we can do about it.”  

Concern ran through their voices, but light--as if addressing a sick dog.

He shifted on what he expected to be a firm mattress under soft covers.
Instead, the surface beneath him was course and gritty, and his cover was rough and weathered.

> _This isn't my cover._
> _This isn't my bed._
> _What is this?_

He strained his eyes open.

> “He's stirring.”  

> “Careful now, don't spook him.”  

> “Here, drink—or at least wet your face.”  

Wooden scraping followed, and a bowl appeared before his face.
He pushed himself up and peered into the bowl, then paused.

Inside was water—not reflecting his face, but rather the wobbling image of pointed ears; sharp, fluffy cheeks; and a slim snout.

> _A dog?_

He squinted, looking harder.

> _A fox._
> _Why do I look like a fox?_

His head tilted confused as he took a hand, furred and clawed, and touched an ear with it.

It twitched on its own.

His face lit up and he touched it again, pleased when it moved.

> “He's really out of it, huh?”
