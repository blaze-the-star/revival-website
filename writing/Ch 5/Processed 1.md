### Section 1 — A Strap of Leather

Morning had already done its work on the camp by the time the first meal was finished—embers stirred, crumbs swept away, gear shifted into neat piles that meant _ready_.

Eli sat off to the side where the ground was flat, legs braced, a one-hand crossbow laid across his lap. He worked without hurry, but without pause—tool in one paw, cord in the other. The bow itself was blunt at the ends from use and travel, the wood darkened where hands had worn it smooth.

A thin sling—fresh leather, still stiff—lay beside it.

He threaded it carefully from the blunt end up toward the tip, testing tension, adjusting the knots so the strap would sit right when thrown over a shoulder. Practical. Quiet. Something meant for moving without fumbling.

Footsteps scuffed behind him.

Fight came up with his usual economy, as if walking too loud would cost him something. He stopped just before Eli, arms crossed.

> “Job’s set,” he said.  
> “Tomorrow. Escort.”

Eli didn’t look up. His claws tugged the last knot snug.

> “Who’s on it?” he asked.

> “Me. Snout. Chatter,” Fight replied.  
> “Kid’s comin’ for learnin’. Snout’s agreed.”

Eli hummed once, then slid the strap through his paw, lifted the crossbow, and tested how it hung. It settled against his side like it belonged there.

> “Bring Joseph too,” he said.

Fight’s ears twitched. He kept his face flat, but the irritation showed anyway—tight in the jaw, sharp in the eyes.

> “Why?” Fight asked, voice flat in that way that tried to sound casual and failed.  
> “He ain’t cut out for escort work. He’s barely cut out for standin’ still.”

Eli finally glanced up.

> “He ain’t actin’ right,” he said simply.  
> “And bein’ left behind ain’t helpin’ him none.”

Fight gave a short, dismissive huff.

> “He’s fine.”

Eli lifted one finger and pointed—not at Fight, but over his shoulder.

Fight’s eyes followed the line.

Across the camp, half-hidden between trees, Joseph stood facing a trunk like it had personally offended him. He tilted forward head first.

Bonk.

He leaned back an inch, then:

Bonk.

Again. Measured. Repeated. As if he could knock a thought loose by impact.

Fight stared a moment.

Then he grunted, conceding without admitting it.

> “...Alright.”

Eli rose, the finished crossbow settling across his shoulder on the new sling. He checked the strap once more.

Fight glanced toward the packs by the fire, then toward the path that led out.

> “I gotta go meet the contact,” he said, tone clipped.  
> “Confirm route, time, all that. Don’t want us walkin’ blind.”

Eli nodded once.

> “Go.”

Fight turned, then paused as if he might add something else. He didn’t. He only grunted and headed down the path, boots scuffing dirt until the trees swallowed him.

As Fight walked off toward the supplies, Eli turned the other way—toward Joseph—his steps steady, his gaze fixed on the fox like he already knew this would take patience.

### Section 2 — Something to Hold

Joseph rubbed the side of his head, wincing slightly.  
He was rubbed at his forehead, wincing faintly like the motion was starting to take a toll.
His ears sat low, not from pain so much as frustration, like he was tired of being himself.

Footsteps crunched nearby.

Joseph looked up—and snapped straight immediately, spine stiff, ears forward, as if he hadn’t just been repeatedly bonking himself against a tree like it owed him answers.

Eli stopped a few paces away, taking in the too-fast correction with a look that said he’d noticed. He didn’t comment on it.

> “We’re runnin’ an escort tomorrow,” Eli said.  
> “I want you comin’ along.”

Joseph blinked.

> “Why?” he asked.  
> “I’m not cut out for that kind of work.”

Eli studied him for a moment, then spoke plainly.

> “I see you strugglin’,” he said.  
> “And sittin’ around camp starin’ at trees ain’t helpin’.”

Joseph’s ears dipped. He looked off to the side, exhaling through his nose.

Silenced filled the air where thought withheld words.

Eli stepped closer and lowered to his haunches. He slipped the crossbow from his shoulder and held it out.
Up close, it was compact—built for one hand, balanced and practical.
In Joseph’s paws, it suddenly looked much larger.

> “Why are you giving me the crossbow?”

Joseph asked, voice tight.  
He glanced at the small hip-quiver Eli had set beside it—short arrows, their ends blunt rather than tipped.

> “And… these?”

Eli set a small hip-quiver beside it—short bolts, their tips blunt and rounded.

> “Somethin’ to get good at,” Eli said.  
> “Somethin’ useful.”

Joseph swallowed.

Eli nodded toward a tree at the edge of the clearing.

> “Go on. Try.”

Joseph’s ears twitched uncertainly. 
He shifted his stance, trying to find balance in a body that still didn’t feel like his. 
He fitted one blunt arrow into place with clumsy care, then lifted the crossbow.

His arms trembled—not from weakness, but unfamiliarity. 
The weight distribution was wrong. 
His grip was wrong. 
His shoulders wrong. 
Everything about it felt wrong.

He aimed anyway.
He pulled.
The string snapped forward.
The arrow flew and thudded into the dirt a full body-length short of the stump.

Joseph stared at where it landed.

Eli’s voice stayed even.

> “Keep practicin’,” he said, already turning.  
> “You’ll take it with you.”

And then he left Joseph there—with the crossbow heavy in his paws.

Joseph stared down at it, heart thudding.

A weapon.

That meant expectation.  
Training.  
A future moment where hesitation wouldn’t be allowed.
**Responsibility.**

The weight of it pressed down on him—not in his arms, but in his chest.

Like a cloud forming overhead.

He stared at the crossbow until his eyes blurred slightly, then blinked hard and looked out toward the stump again.

His paws tightened.
He loaded another blunt arrow.
He raised the weapon.
And tried to believe that being prepared wasn’t the same thing as being condemned.
