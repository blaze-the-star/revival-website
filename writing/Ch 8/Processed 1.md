Chatter leaned closer, squinting past Joseph as if something had just clicked.

“Hey—wait. Isn’t that the fox from the alley, Joseph?”

Joseph’s ears flattened instantly.

He turned a sharp, silent glare on Chatter—wide-eyed, urgent, a wordless _don’t_.

Snout blinked between them.  
“The alley?”

Joseph’s mouth opened, then closed.  
“I—it was nothing,” he said, too quickly. “I just… dropped my cover. She gave it back.”

Crystal’s head snapped toward him.

Just for a moment.

Her eyes widened—surprise flashing across her face—then she looked away again, attention returning pointedly to the table, to her bowl, to anything but him.

Snout’s expression shifted.

Recognition settled in, slow and unmistakable.

“Ohhh,” she said.

Joseph shot a glance toward Crystal—too fast to catch anything useful—then turned back to Snout, brow furrowing.

“Why did you say it like that?”

Snout’s ears twitched, just slightly.  
“Because most folks wouldn’t do that.”

Joseph looked around the table. At Chatter. At Snout. At the strangers who had absolutely stopped pretending not to listen.

“…Why do I feel like there’s something you’re not telling me?”

For a beat, no one answered.

Then Crystal lifted a paw to her mouth and laughed—quiet, contained, the sound slipping out before she could quite stop it.

She didn’t explain.

And somehow, that made it worse.
