```
- Joseph, Snout, and Chatter walk down the street of Flint Water
- Snout has Joseph walk in front
- Joseph protests, saying he doesn't know where they're going
- Snout tells him that she'll tell him when to turn

- The group follows Snout's directions to the gathered bowl.
```

Flint Water’s main street sloped toward the river, and tucked between a cooper’s shop and a chandler’s stall sat **The Gathered Bowl**.  
Smoke curled from its chimney, carrying the rich scent of stew and baked grain. Inside, long wooden tables ran the length of the room, crowded with animals of all kinds. There were no private booths—just shared space and the low, warm hum of community.

Snout led them in without hesitation. Joseph paused in the doorway, his ears tilting.

> “We’re… sitting with strangers?”

> “Of course,” Snout said. “Why not?”

> “Where I’m from, you usually sit alone. With your own group.”

Chatter blinked up at him.

> “Really? That sounds lonely.”

Snout shrugged.

> “Different ways. Here, you share space unless there’s a fight brewing.”

She chose a spot near the middle where a pair of older badgers and a young raccoon were already eating.  
Snout settled onto a floor cushion, leaving two wooden stools for Joseph and Chatter.

Joseph eyed his stool—it stood higher, balanced unevenly. He climbed onto it carefully, tail coiled for balance. Even seated, he was still shorter than Snout. He felt perched like a child in a high chair.

Chatter hopped onto his own stool with otter-ish grace and immediately leaned toward the nearest badger.

> “What’s in the stew today?”

> “Roots, river fish, thyme,” the badger grunted. “Tastes better than it sounds.”

Joseph kept his gaze down, adjusting his coat sleeves. That’s when a scent caught him—warm earth, frost, that faint fruity sweetness.

His head turned before he could stop it.

There, three tables over, stood Crystal.  
The red fox was watching him. When their eyes met, she didn’t look away—just tilted her head slightly.

Joseph twitched, and the stool shifted under him. One leg lifted off the floor, and he jerked forward, clutching the table edge.  
He didn’t look back again.

> “You alright?” Snout asked, her voice low.

> “Fine,” Joseph muttered. “Stool’s uneven.”

> “They all are,” said one of the badgers without looking up. “Builds character.”

A moment later, a shadow fell across their corner of the table.  
Crystal pulled over a stool from another spot and set it down beside Joseph—close enough that her sleeve brushed his.

She sat as though she’d been invited.

Joseph stared rigidly at his empty bowl.

Chatter’s eyes went wide.

> “Joseph—isn't that the fox from the alley!”

Joseph’s ears flattened.  
He shot Chatter a glare that could have cut through ice.

Snout’s gaze sharpened.

> “The alley?”

> “It was nothing,” Joseph said too quickly.  
> “I dropped my blanket. She gave it back.”

Crystal glanced at him, a flicker of surprise—then smoothed her expression and studied the menu board as if it were fascinating.

Snout looked between them, her coyote-smile slow and knowing.

> “Ohhh.”

> “Why’d you say it like that?” Joseph hissed.

> “Most others wouldn't have done that.”

Joseph’s eyes darted around the table. The badgers were pretending not to listen. The raccoon had paused, spoon halfway to his mouth.

> “Why do I feel like there’s something you’re not telling me?” Joseph whispered.

Crystal covered her mouth with one paw, a quiet laugh escaping.

---

The stew arrived. Joseph ate without tasting it. Crystal stayed beside him, a quiet, graceful intrusion.

After a while, she spoke, low enough that only he could hear.

> “You listen more than you pretend not to.”

Joseph kept his eyes on his bowl.

> “I listen to everything. That doesn’t mean I like you.”

> “I didn’t say you liked me.”

> “Well, I don’t,” he stammered. “So what’s your point?”

She smiled.

> “No point. Just an observation.”

Chatter leaned in.

> “Joseph doesn’t like a lot of things here. He wants to go home.”

Crystal’s ears perked.

> “Home?”

Joseph set his spoon down.

> “I don’t plan on staying here long. I just need to find a way back.”

Her playful expression softened. She studied him—the tight set of his shoulders, the way his paws worried at the table’s edge.

> “Where is home?”

> “Far,” he said quietly. “Somewhere else.”

He couldn’t explain further. Not here. Not with strangers listening.  
And not to her—someone who already seemed to see too much.

Snout watched them both, saying nothing, but her ears were angled forward, taking in every word.  
Her mind, however, was elsewhere—back on the patrol route, back to Soot’s stall, to the way the black fox had smiled when she said, _Say hello to the lil’ fox for me._

Snout hadn’t told Eli.  
She’d called it “uneventful.”  
Now, watching Joseph shrink under Crystal’s attention, she wondered if she’d made a mistake by saying nothing.

Crystal shifted on her stool.

> “If you’re just passing through,” she said, teasing but careful, “then there’s no harm in letting someone be nice to you while you’re here.”

> “I didn’t ask for nice.”

> “You didn’t have to.”

Joseph finally looked at her—really looked. Her eyes were amber in the lantern light, sharp and clever, but not unkind.  
For a heartbeat, he felt that old, familiar caution rise—the worry that anyone this persistent might pull him away from the quiet place where he met God.

But beneath that caution was another feeling, one he refused to name: the faint, unwelcome warmth of being seen.

He looked away first.

> “I’m not staying,” he repeated, as much to himself as to her.

> “I heard you the first time,” Crystal said softly. Then, almost to herself, “Doesn’t mean I have to ignore you until you leave.”

Chatter grinned.

> “I like her.”

> “Hush,” Joseph and Snout said at the same time.

Crystal laughed—bright and unguarded.

Joseph’s ears twitched. He didn’t smile back, but the tightness in his chest eased, just a little.

---

As they ate, the door to The Gathered Bowl opened again.  
A lean, travel-worn coyote slipped inside—not a local. He moved to the far end of the room, where a figure in a hooded cloak sat in shadow.  
Snout’s eyes tracked them. She couldn’t hear the exchange, but she saw coins pass under the table. A small scroll changed paws.

_Eli’s been looking for contracts_, she thought. _But that wasn’t a posting board. That was a handoff._

Crystal followed her gaze, then leaned toward Joseph, her voice dropping to a whisper.

> “You travel with a wolf, a coyote, a bobcat, and an otter. Quite the collection.”

Joseph stiffened.

> “How do you know who I travel with?”

> “Flint Water’s small. Eli’s reputation isn’t.” She paused. “Neither is Soot’s.”

The name landed like a stone.  
Snout’s ears snapped forward.

> “You know Soot?”

> “Everyone knows Soot,” Crystal said lightly. “She asks questions. Sells remedies. Watches everything.” Her eyes returned to Joseph. “She’s been asking about a fox who didn’t bow.”

Joseph’s breath caught. The stew turned to clay in his stomach.

Snout’s earlier unease tightened into a cold knot. She’d hoped Soot’s curiosity was idle. Now it had a witness.

> “What did you tell her?” Snout asked, her voice carefully level.

> “Nothing,” Crystal said. “I don’t work for her. I don’t work for the shrine, either.” She looked at Joseph again. “But if she’s looking for you, she’ll find you. She’s good at finding things people try to hide.”

Joseph pushed his bowl away. His appetite was gone, replaced by the old, familiar pressure—the sense of being hunted in a world he didn’t understand.

> “Why are you telling me this?” he whispered.

Crystal’s expression sobered.

> “Because you stood when everyone else knelt. And you’re still here.” She hesitated. “That either makes you very brave or very lost. Either way… you shouldn’t face Soot alone.”

> “I’m not alone,” Joseph said, but the words sounded small.

> “I know,” Crystal said. “You have them.” She glanced at Snout. “And maybe… you have me, too. If you want.”

Snout stood abruptly, her stool scraping the floor.

> “We should go. Eli’s waiting.”

Joseph stood too quickly—his stool wobbled, and Crystal’s paw shot out, steadying his elbow before he could tip.  
He pulled away as soon as he was balanced, ears burning.

> “Thanks,” he mumbled.

> “Any time,” she said, and this time her smile was small, genuine.

As they left The Gathered Bowl, Joseph didn’t look back. But he felt her gaze on him until the door closed behind them.

Outside, the evening air was cool. Chatter skipped ahead, already retelling the meal to an imaginary audience.  
Snout walked beside Joseph, silent for a long moment.

> “She’s clever,” Snout said at last. “And she knows more than she let on.”

> “Do you think she’s dangerous?”

> “I think she’s a player. And I think Soot’s closer than I told Eli.”

Joseph pulled his coat tighter.

> “What do we do?”

> “We tell Eli,” Snout said, her voice grim. “And we don’t come back to town until we know who’s watching, and why.”

As they walked back toward camp, Flint Water’s lights glowing behind them, Joseph felt the night press in from all sides.  
Crystal’s amber eyes. Soot’s unseen gaze. The hooded figure in the tavern. Eli’s unspoken rules.

And beneath it all, the quiet, persistent whisper:

_You don’t belong here.  
But you have nowhere else to go._

He walked a little faster, keeping close to Snout’s side, and wondered when the world had become a thing you survived instead of a place you lived.
