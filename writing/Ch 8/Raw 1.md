Snout glanced between them.  
“You two know each other?”

Joseph stiffened.  
“We—uh—briefly. I dropped my cover and she gave it back.”

Snout nodded, knowingly.  
“Ohh.”

Joseph frowned.  
“Why did you say it like that?”

Snout’s ears twitched, just slightly.  
“Most folks wouldn't have done that.”

“It was cold. Anyone decent would’ve done the same.”

Joseph looks at her eyebrows furled--unconvinced, but unable to argue.

Chatter scratched behind one ear.  
“I mean—it _was_ kinda rough. You ran off, and next thing I know he’s curled up in an alley like he lost a fight with—”

“Chatter,” Joseph said too quickly.  
“Maybe you should say less. Please.”

Chatter’s mouth snapped shut.  
“Oh. Yeah. Sorry.”

Crystal watched the exchange with quiet interest.

“He didn’t look like he needed an audience,” she said mildly.

Food arrived soon after.  
Bowls were set down, bread passed hand to hand.  
The table settled into the soft clatter of dishes and steam.

Joseph lowered his head.  
He brought his paws together, fingers curling inward, and whispered—so quietly it barely carried past the space in front of him.

“Blessed are You, Father in Heaven, King of the universe,  
who brings forth bread from the earth.

May Your will be done in the lives of those who made this food,  
and may they be blessed.

In Yeshua’s name. Amen.”

He stayed like that a moment longer, then reached for his bowl.

No one spoke.

Crystal watched him over the rim of her dish, expression unreadable, and then—deliberately—looked away.

They ate in silence for a while after that.  
Steam curled from the bowls. Spoons scraped softly against clay.

Chatter was the first to crack.  
“I’m sayin’, if this is what passes for ‘simple fare,’ I’m not complainin’.”

Snout hummed.  
“You complained the whole way here.”

“I complained _about walking_.”

Crystal listened, finishing her meal without hurry.

“The roads have been calmer lately,” she said at last.

Eli shrugged.  
“Means trouble’s gatherin’ somewhere else.”

She nodded, accepting that.  

The table had settled into a quieter rhythm by then.
“You’ll find people here don’t mean half of what they say,” Crystal said, almost idly.

Joseph answered without looking up.  
“I think that’s true everywhere.”

Crystal smiled—small, unreadable.
