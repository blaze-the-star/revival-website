Soot ignored the question and walked.

Joseph blinked, then hurried after her, his shorter legs working to keep up. The path led steadily downhill, the trees thinning as the ground grew more even. Before long, the first buildings of Flint Water emerged from the darkness—hunched shapes of wood and stone, their windows dark, their doorways empty.

Soot didn't slow.

She led him through the sleeping town with the confidence of someone who knew exactly where the night watch wouldn't be looking, which alleys swallowed sound, which doorways offered shadow. Joseph followed in silence, his paws raw against the cold stone, breath misting in the chill air.

They emerged into a small square—empty now, its stalls shuttered, its fountain still. The moon hung directly overhead, painting everything in pale silver and deep black.

Soot stopped in the center and turned to face him.

> "Alright," she said. "Here."

Joseph looked around. The square was exposed—anyone passing could see them—but no one was passing. The town held its breath around them, waiting for morning.

> "Why here?"

> "Because no one's listening," Soot replied. "And if they are, they're too far to hear clearly."

She sat on the edge of the fountain, tail curling around her paws. Her dark fur absorbed the moonlight, making her look like a hole cut into the night.

Joseph hesitated, then sat across from her, the fountain's stone cold even through his coat.

Soot watched him for a long moment.

Then she spoke.

> "Tell me about your God."
