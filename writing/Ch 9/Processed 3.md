Joseph woke to stillness.

Not the ordinary stillness of late night—the kind that settled over camp like a blanket, soft and familiar. This was something else. A quiet that felt _held_. Like the world had stopped breathing and was waiting to see what happened next.

He lay curled in his tent, blanket pulled to his chin, coat folded beneath his head as a makeshift pillow. His ears twitched. The canvas above him held the faint silver glow of moonlight, enough to trace the seam where the tent flap lay closed.

Nothing moved.

But something was wrong.

He didn't know how he knew. There was no sound, no rustle of footsteps, no shift of shadow against the canvas. Just… a pressure. A presence. The sense that he was not alone.

Joseph held very still.

His heartbeat quickened, but he forced his breathing to stay even, shallow—pretending sleep, pretending unawareness. His ears strained, swiveling independently, searching the darkness for anything his eyes couldn't find.

And then—

A whisper of movement. The faintest brush of fabric against grass.

Someone was _inside_ the tent.

Joseph's body went rigid beneath the blanket. His mind raced through options—scream, run, fight—but his limbs felt locked, frozen by the sheer impossibility of the moment. No one from camp would enter without warning. No one from camp would move like this, silent as smoke.

A paw touched his shoulder.

He flinched—hard—and scrambled backward, blanket tangling around his legs, a yelp catching in his throat—

"Hush."

The voice was low, urgent, female. A paw pressed gently but firmly against his chest, not hurting, just _stopping_.

"Be still. I'm not here to hurt you."

Joseph's eyes adjusted to the dark shape before him—slim, upright, the faint outline of ears and muzzle barely visible against the canvas. The scent reached him then: earth, moss, something sharp and green he couldn't name. Not one of the pack. Not anyone he knew.

"Who—" he started.

"Quiet," she breathed. "You want them to find me here?"

Joseph swallowed hard, heart slamming against his ribs. He stared at the silhouette, trying to make sense of it—trying to find the shape of threat or safety in the darkness.

The figure shifted, leaning closer. Her voice dropped to barely a whisper.

"My name's Pearl. I need to talk to you. And we can't do it here."

Pearl.

The name flickered somewhere in Joseph's memory—but not clearly. Not the way Snout's name did, or Fight's, or Chatter's. Just a shape without edges.

"I don't—" he started.

"I know you don't know me," she cut in softly. "But I know _you_. The shrine walk. The wind. The God you wouldn't bow for."

Joseph's breath caught.

"I've been looking for you," she went on. "Not to hurt you. Just to…" She hesitated, the first crack in her certainty. "Just to understand."

The tent flap rustled. Voices drifted from the main camp—low, indistinct. Fight, maybe, shifting on watch.

Pearl went completely still. Joseph felt the tension in her posture, the way her body coiled like a spring, ready to vanish if the voices came closer.

They didn't. The night settled again.

Pearl exhaled slowly, then turned back to him.

"I can get you out without them hearing. But you have to trust me." A pause. "Can you do that?"

Joseph stared at her in the darkness.

Every instinct told him to call out. To wake Snout. To let Eli handle this stranger who'd slipped into his tent like smoke through cracks.

But something held him back.

Not trust, exactly. Not yet.

Curiosity.

Because she'd said _understand_. Because she'd come for him in the middle of the night, risked everything, just to _talk_. Because the word _God_ had crossed her lips like a question she couldn't put down.

Slowly, carefully, Joseph freed himself from the blanket.

"...Okay," he whispered.

Pearl moved before he finished speaking—smooth, practiced, silent. She eased the tent flap open just enough to slip through, then reached back, motioning for him to follow.

Joseph crawled out after her.

The night air hit him cold and sharp. The camp lay quiet, tents dark, the fire pit a bed of embers glowing faintly in the dark. Fight's silhouette stood at the far edge of the clearing, back turned, facing the road.

Pearl pressed close to Joseph's side, her voice a thread of sound.

"Stay low. Move when I move. Don't look back."

She crept forward, keeping to shadow, and Joseph followed.

Every step felt too loud. Every breath a betrayal. The grass rustled beneath his paws, the cold bit through his coat, and his heart hammered so hard he was certain Fight would hear it from across the clearing.

But no shout came. No movement. No alarm.

They reached the treeline.

Pearl pulled him into the darkness between trunks, and they kept moving—faster now, putting distance between themselves and the camp until the glow of the embers vanished and the world became only trees and moonlight and the sound of their own breathing.

Only when they were deep enough that Joseph couldn't have found his way back alone did Pearl finally slow.

She stopped in a small clearing, moonlit and still, and turned to face him.

Joseph stood there, coat clutched around himself, trembling from cold and adrenaline and the sheer strangeness of what he'd just done.

Pearl watched him for a long moment.

Then she said, simply:

"You came."

They didn't speak at first.

Pearl led him through the trees at a steady pace—not running, but purposeful, as though she'd walked this route a hundred times before. Joseph followed close behind, too shaken to ask where they were going, too tired to do anything but put one paw in front of the other.

The cold bit through his coat. He hadn't grabbed his blanket. Hadn't thought to. His ears ached, his nose ran, and his legs—still not fully his own—trembled with every step.

Pearl glanced back once, noted his shivering, and said nothing.

They emerged from the trees onto the road to Flint Water. The town lay ahead, dark and quiet under the stars, its buildings huddled together like sleepers sharing warmth. No lights burned. No voices carried. Just the low murmur of the river and the distant creak of a sign swinging in the wind.

Joseph's chest tightened.

_Flint Water._ The place Eli had barred him from. The place where priests raised staves and crowds knelt in unison.

He stopped walking.

Pearl noticed immediately. She turned, brow furrowing.

"What?"

"I'm not supposed to be here," Joseph said quietly.

Pearl's expression didn't change.

"I know."

She said it simply. Not dismissively. Not cruelly. Just… acknowledging.

Then she walked back to him, stopping close enough that he could smell the strange green sharpness clinging to her fur.

"Eli's trying to protect you," she said. "I get that. But I'm not gonna hurt you." A pause. "And I'm not gonna let anyone else hurt you either. Not tonight."

Joseph stared at her.

"Why should I believe that?"

She met his gaze without flinching.

"You shouldn't. Not yet." She tilted her head slightly. "But you're here anyway. So either you're desperate, or some part of you trusts me already."

Joseph opened his mouth to argue—and found he couldn't.

Because she was right. Some part of him had trusted her. Enough to follow. Enough to leave the only safety he'd found in this world.

He didn't know what that said about him.

But as the thought settled, something else rose beneath it—cool and clear, like water finding its level.

*Wait.*

He looked at her again. Really looked this time.

The way she'd appeared in his tent. The way she'd moved—silent, practiced, like someone who'd done this before. The way she'd spoken just now: so either you're desperate, or some part of you trusts me already.

It was a good line. A convincing line.

A *manipulative* line.

Joseph's eyes narrowed.

He took a small step backward.

Pearl's brow furrowed slightly. "What—"

Another step.

"What are you doing?"

Joseph kept his voice level—quieter than hers, but steady.

"Trying to figure out if I made a mistake."

Pearl's ears flicked back. "I told you, I'm not going to hurt you."

"You told me a lot of things." Another step. The distance between them widened. "But you haven't shown me anything. Except that you can get into tents without waking anyone."

Pearl's jaw tightened. "I masked my scent. So I wouldn't spook the others. That's not—"

"That's not nothing." Joseph shook his head slowly. "That's planning. That's skill. You knew exactly what you were doing."

He took another step back.

Pearl's tail went still.

"What do you want me to say?" Her voice sharpened—just slightly, the first crack in her control. "That I'm dangerous? Fine. I'm dangerous. I've been dangerous for years. That doesn't mean I'm lying about why I came."

"Then show me."

Joseph stopped moving. He stood in the middle of the road, small and shivering, coat clutched around himself—but something in his posture had shifted. A stillness that wasn't fear.

"Convince me," he said quietly. "Not with words. Not with reasons. Tell me something real. Something you don't want me to know."

Pearl stared at him.

The silence stretched.

Somewhere in the distance, an owl called.

And Joseph waited.

Not defiantly. Not aggressively. Just… present. Like he had all the time in the world.

Like he wasn't the one being led.

Like he held something she needed more than she held anything over him.

Pearl's expression flickered—through several things Joseph couldn't quite name—before settling on something raw and unguarded that made his chest ache.

"You're supposed to follow," she said quietly. "You're supposed to just—" She broke off, shaking her head. "That's how this works. I lead. You trust. That's the deal."

Joseph shook his head slowly.

"I didn't agree to any deal."

Pearl's voice dropped. "Then why'd you come?"

It wasn't a challenge anymore. It was a question—genuine, uncertain, almost fragile.

Joseph met her eyes.

"I've been asking myself that since we left the trees."

Another step back.

"And if you can't give me a reason to stay—something real—then I'm leaving."

Pearl's ears flattened.

"You can't. You don't even know the way back."

"Then I'll wander until I find it." Joseph's voice stayed calm. No heat. No threat. Just truth. "I'd rather be lost in the dark than follow someone who's only showing me what they want me to see."

He took another step.

Pearl's paw lifted—not reaching for him, just… moving, like she didn't know what to do with her own body.

"That's not—" She stopped. Swallowed. "You don't understand."

"Then help me understand."

Another step back.

Pearl's breath came sharper now. Quicker. The mask was cracking—he could see it in the way her eyes darted, the way her tail curled tight against her legs.

"I can't just—" She shook her head. "I don't do this. I don't stand in roads and—"

"Then walk away." Joseph's voice was gentle. "Go back to Flint Water. Forget you found me. I won't tell anyone."

He meant it.

And somehow, she knew.

Pearl stood frozen, caught between the town and the road, between the story she'd prepared and the reality of this small, stubborn fox who wouldn't be led.

"Tell me," Joseph said softly. "Not what you want me to know. Not what you think will work. Tell me the truth about why you came."

Another step back.

The distance between them was real now. Joseph could feel the cold seeping through his coat, the weight of the dark pressing around him. If she didn't speak—if she let him walk—he would.

He was sure of it.

Pearl's voice came broken.

"Because I'm tired."

Joseph stopped.

Pearl stood there, small against the night, and for the first time she looked exactly what she was—not a threat, not a manipulator, not a survivor with years of practice.

Just tired.

So tired.

"I'm tired of being Soot," she whispered. "I'm tired of being the one everyone watches. The one everyone expects to fail." Her voice cracked. "I'm tired of knowing I did something right and watching it burn everything I loved."

Joseph didn't move.

Didn't step closer.

But he didn't step back either.

Pearl's eyes were wet. She didn't wipe them.

"I saw you in that square," she said. "I saw you stand there like you had something nothing could touch. And I—" She shook her head sharply. "I wanted that. I wanted to know if it was real. If He was real."

She looked at him directly.

"Not because I'm good. Not because I deserve it. Because I'm tired of there being nothing."

The words hung between them.

Joseph breathed.

Then, slowly—so slowly—he walked forward.

Not all the way. Just close enough that she could see his face in the starlight.

"Okay," he said quietly. "I'll come with you."

Pearl blinked—like she hadn't expected that. Like some part of her had braced for rejection.

"Why?" The word came out rough, almost defensive. "I just told you—I'm Soot. I'm the one everyone—"

"I heard you," Joseph interrupted gently. "You told me who they made you. Not who you are."

Pearl stared at him.

For a moment, she looked utterly lost—like no one had ever said anything like that to her before.

Then she turned sharply, facing the road to Flint Water.

"Come on," she muttered. "It's cold, and I know a place with a fire."

She started walking.

Joseph followed.

But this time, he walked beside her.
