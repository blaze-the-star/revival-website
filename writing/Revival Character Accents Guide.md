Best practice:
- Light accent cues early in scenes
- Let rhythm carry the rest 
- Reintroduce markers under stress or emotion

### Snout — _Frontier-Plain, Guard-Casual_
**Vibe:**  
Practical, steady, understated. Sounds like someone who grew up around people who _don’t waste breath_, but isn’t rough or uneducated.

Think: frontier-adjacent, communal, functional—**not** Southern, not Western, not medieval.

#### Core Traits
- Short, complete sentences
- Rare contractions when being serious
- Drops softeners (“well,” “I guess”) unless comforting someone
- Calm declaratives instead of questions
- Uses _plain verbs_ over decorative ones

She speaks like someone used to being listened to.

#### Accent Markers
- Minimal slang
- Occasional clipped phrasing
- Doesn’t hedge unless emotionally careful
- Rarely raises her voice; authority comes from certainty

#### Avoid
- Heavy dialect spelling
- Drawls or folksy phrasing
- Overuse of “ain’t” or regional markers

#### Example
> “We’ll stay the night.”  
> “No sense pushing in the dark.”  
> “You’re safe. Sit down.”

When she _softens_:
> “Hey. It’s alright. I’ve got you.”

### Fight — _Blunt Rural, Slightly Abraded_

**Vibe:**  
Same cultural origin as Snout, but worn down by conflict. His speech is heavier, more compressed, occasionally rough around the edges.

He sounds like someone who stopped explaining himself years ago.

#### Core Traits
- Truncated sentences
- Drops subjects (“Don’t like it.” instead of “I don’t like it.”)
- Occasional hard consonants
- Speaks less overall; when he speaks, it lands

#### Accent Markers
- Contractions are functional, not casual
- Rare humor, usually dry
- Lets silence do work
- Uses _physical metaphors_ unconsciously

#### Avoid
- Making him sound angry all the time
- Overusing grunts or growls
- Turning him into a stereotype “tough guy”

#### Example
> “Doesn’t feel right.”  
> “We move at first light.”  
> “If they come back, I’ll hear it.”

When emotional:
> “Don’t push him.”  
> “He’s had enough.”

### Eli — _Learned Native + Human Leakage_
**This one is important.**

**Vibe:**  
Eli mostly passes as local—but under stress, authority, or introspection, **human cadence leaks through**.

He doesn’t sound _foreign_.  
He sounds… _slightly misaligned_.

#### Core Traits
- More complex sentence structures than natives
- Uses abstract phrasing natives don’t
- Occasionally too precise
- Speaks carefully, like he’s translating in his head

#### Accent Markers (Human Leakage)

These should appear:
- when explaining ideas
- when recalling Earth
- when emotionally vulnerable
- when speaking to Joseph

Examples of leakage:
- “Look—what I’m saying is—”
- “That’s not how this works.”
- “You don’t understand the implications.”

Then he corrects himself.

#### Avoid
- Making him sound modern-casual all the time
- Slang
- Internet-era phrasing

He’s 1995 human, not 2025.

#### Example

Passing as native:
> “We’ll stay put tonight.”  
> “I’ll talk to them in the morning.”

Leakage:
> “You can’t just—no. That’s not how this plays out.”  
> “Back where I’m from—” (cuts himself off)

With Joseph:
> “Listen to me. You have to be careful here.”  
> “I made mistakes you don’t get to repeat.”

### Fight — _Inherited Native + Human Echo_
**Core Concept:**  
Fight’s accent is **mostly native**, but it’s _not clean_. It bears the imprint of Eli’s speech patterns from the years when Fight was still learning how to talk like an adult.

So instead of “rough local,” Fight sounds:
- grounded
- restrained
- slightly off-rhythm compared to others
- occasionally _too structured_ for someone who grew up fully native

It’s subtle—but consistent.

#### How Fight Sounds, Broadly
Fight’s voice is:
- quieter than Snout’s
- heavier than Chatter’s
- more _measured_ than either

He speaks like someone who learned **how to talk from someone thinking carefully**, not casually.

#### Accent Traits
##### 1. Sentence Structure: Slightly Over-Organized
Fight sometimes uses:
- clearer sentence logic than other natives
- cause-and-effect phrasing
- “if/then” thinking

This is the human influence.

Example:
> “If they come back, we’ll hear it.”

That’s not native-short.  
That’s _learned clarity_.

##### 2. Controlled Contractions
Fight uses contractions—but **selectively**.
- He contracts when comfortable
- He expands when serious or emotional

Example:
> “Don’t like this.”  
> vs.  
> “I do not think that’s safe.”

That expansion is a tell.

##### 3. Emotional Compression (Not Inarticulation)
He’s not inarticulate—he’s _contained_.

He doesn’t trail off.  
He _finishes_, then stops.

Example:
> “We shouldn’t stay.”  
> “That’s all.”
##### 4. Vocabulary: Practical, Slightly Abstract
Compared to Snout:
- Snout says _what to do_
- Fight sometimes says _why_

That’s Eli.

Example:
> “Doesn’t add up.”  
> “Feels wrong.”

Both are native—but the second is more humanly interpretive.

#### Where the Human Influence Shows Most
Fight sounds _most human_ when:
- protecting someone
- making a moral call
- disagreeing with Eli (ironically)

That’s because those are the moments he learned speech from Eli directly.

Example:
> “You can’t do that.”  
> “It’s not right.”

That moral framing is human, not native.

#### What NOT to Do With Fight
- Don’t make him growly or brutish
- Don’t make him elliptical to the point of confusion
- Don’t give him modern slang
- Don’t over-explain his thoughts in dialogue

His depth comes from _precision_, not volume.

#### Contrast With Eli (Important)
This pairing matters:
- **Eli**: human trying to sound native
- **Fight**: native shaped by human speech

So sometimes Fight will sound:
- _more native_ than Eli
- _more human_ than Eli
- or unsettlingly similar

That’s good.

Example exchange:
> **Eli:** “We need to think this through.”  
> **Fight:** “I already did.”

Short. Clean. Human-shaped.

#### Sample Dialogue (Revised Fight)

Casual:

> “Camp’s quiet tonight.”  
> “Too quiet.”

Concerned:

> “I don’t like how this feels.”  
> “Something’s off.”

Protective:

> “Leave him alone.”  
> “He’s not ready.”

With Joseph:

> “You remind me of him.”  
> (Does not explain.)

### Chatter — _Riverborn, Fast-Tongued, Youthful_
**Vibe:**  
The most _audibly_ accented—but in rhythm, not spelling. He talks fast, stacks clauses, and fills silence instinctively.

Think: river culture, communal upbringing, expressive but not dumb.

#### Core Traits
- Overlapping thoughts
- Casual contractions
- Uses filler phrases
- Talks _through_ ideas while forming them
- Emotionally transparent

#### Accent Markers
- “I mean—”
- “You know?”
- “Like—okay, but—”
- Stacks verbs: “I was gonna try and see if—”

#### Avoid
- Turning him into comic relief only
- Excessive misspellings
- Making him sound childish when he’s serious

#### Example
> “Okay, look, I’m just sayin’, that was weird.”  
> “I mean—not bad weird. Just… weird weird.”

When scared:
> “Hey—hey—no, that’s not funny.”  
> “Joseph. Joseph, look at me.”
